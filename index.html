03 — Case Files

Four datasets, four decisions

Each project follows the same discipline: find the problem in the noise, validate it with a significance test, and hand back a call the business can make.

CASE 01 / RETAIL

VendorIQ & Inventory Analytics

Problem
Vendor and inventory data was fragmented across tables, hiding where working capital was actually stuck.
Approach
Modeled 6 relational tables — 15.6M rows, 129 vendors, 268K products — into a star schema powering 48 business queries.
Finding
$44.85M was tied up in high-value inventory outliers across 901 SKUs (9.33% of the catalog), confirmed with statistical testing in Python (SciPy).
Deliverable
3-page Power BI dashboard showing 15 vendors (12% of the supplier base) drive 37.73% of revenue, plus a 32.6% unit-cost reduction from bulk purchasing (Mann–Whitney U, p < 0.001).

CASE 02 / HEALTHCARE

Healthcare Operations & Financial Performance

Problem
Hospital admission and billing records needed to be trustworthy before any cost driver could go in front of stakeholders.
Approach
Cleaned and standardized 54,966 admission records (from 55,500 raw rows) across 28 SQL business queries.
Finding
106 patient records carried negative billing — a data-quality issue costing up to $2,008 each — surfaced by decomposing the admissions data in Python.
Deliverable
Cost drivers validated with t-test, ANOVA, and chi-square testing before reaching a stakeholder-facing billing dashboard.

CASE 03 / FINANCE

Banking Intelligence: Customer & Transaction Analytics

Problem
The bank needed to know which factors actually drove transaction value, not just which ones correlated with it.
Approach
Segmented 10,000+ transactions across 100 customers and ran 18 SQL business queries to profile risk by customer.
Finding
Transaction frequency was the dominant driver of value (r = 0.99); t-test, ANOVA, and chi-square testing ruled out type, channel, and status as significant (p > 0.05 across all three).
Deliverable
3-page Power BI dashboard tracking transaction channels, product mix, and customer-status trends for cross-functional review.
SQL ServerPythonPower BI

CASE 04 / E-COMMERCE

Seller Concentration & Freight Risk Analysis

Problem
A 100K+ order marketplace needed to know how concentrated its seller risk really was.
Approach
Joined 9 relational tables across the marketplace and quantified a 16.57% aggregate freight-to-revenue ratio.
Finding
The top 10% of sellers generate 67.5% of platform revenue; cross-state shipments cost significantly more in freight than same-state (Welch's t-test, p < 0.001).
Deliverable
5 Power BI dashboards translating seller and freight risk into business recommendations.
